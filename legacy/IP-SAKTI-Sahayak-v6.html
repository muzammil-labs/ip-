Every answerhas a clause behind it.

IP-SAKTI Sahayak tells an Ayurveda innovator what their product legally is, what they can protect, what they owe under biodiversity law, and what they may claim — with the exact section, rule or treaty article under every sentence. India and international answers are never mixed. When the evidence runs out, it says so and hands you to a human.

What makes it different

It classifies before it advises

Three to five questions decide whether your product is a classical drug, proprietary medicine, new drug, phytopharmaceutical, Ayurveda Aahara or cosmetic. Every IP and ABS answer depends on that.

Two answers, never one blend

India and international law sit in separate columns with separate citations, so a PCT rule is never mistaken for an Indian one.

Confidence you can audit

Confidence is computed from source authority, coverage and agreement between sources, not from the model's opinion of itself. Disputes are shown, not smoothed over.

It knows when the law moved

Every source carries a version and status. Rule 170 was omitted, stayed, then un-stayed in just over a year; answers built on stale text are flagged.

It stops and hands over

Out-of-scope or unanswerable questions are declined with the missing facts listed, and a one-tap route to a human IP facilitator.

        How IP-SAKTI earns trust

        Guardrails are part of the pipeline, not a disclaimer at the end. This page shows each check, how quality will be measured, and the live audit trail of this session.

        Every answer passes these checks in order

        1. Scope gateMedical dosing, litigation strategy and non-Ayurveda questions are declined.
        2. ClassifyProduct category fixed before any IP or ABS reasoning.
        3. RetrieveHybrid search filtered by jurisdiction, status and date.
        4. DraftModel writes only from the retrieved evidence pack.
        5. Verify citationsEach sentence is re-checked against its cited passage; unsupported sentences are cut.
        6. Conflict and statusDisagreeing sources and superseded law are surfaced.
        7. Abstain or escalateToo little evidence means no answer, plus the missing facts.
